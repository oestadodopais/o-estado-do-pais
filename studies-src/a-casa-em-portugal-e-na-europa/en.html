Housing in Portugal and in Europe

What a home costs in Portugal, how much it rose, who pays for it and who is left out, read in Eurostat's figures for the twenty-seven countries of the European Union, in the National Statistics Institute's (the INE) and in the Bank of Portugal's; what the State does with the 2027 Budget and with this year's laws; what the parties propose and what the street asked for in October 2026, each thing with its document.

In short

Net of inflation, the price index stands at 206.41 in 2025, with 2015 equal to one hundred, against 124.75 in the Union; it is the highest value of the twenty-seven. In the second quarter of 2026 prices rose 16.5 % in a year, against 4.7 % in the Union, again the highest rise of the twenty-seven. Against household income, prices stand at 144.34 (2015 equal to one hundred) against 105.72 in the Union, also the highest value. A home sold in Portugal cost 2,337 euros per square metre in Q1 2026, in Lisbon 5,292 and in Porto 3,729; a new rent, 10.17 euros per square metre in the country, 17.79 in Lisbon, 14.66 in Porto.

And yet the average Portuguese household spends on housing a smaller share of its income than the Union average (16.3 % against 18.9 %) and the share of the population overburdened, more than forty percent of income on housing, is smaller (6.3 % against 7.7 %). 71.2 % of the Portuguese live in their own home, and 39.7 % in their own home with no loan. Among tenants at market rents, 27.2 % are overburdened, against 18.6 % in the Union, and they were 19.7 % in 2020; and the young leave their parents' home at 28.8 years on average, against 26.3 in the Union, with 42.1 % of those aged twenty-five to thirty-four living with their parents, against 30.1 %.

The State answers with 2,003 million euros in the 2027 housing programme, with reduced value added tax (VAT, IVA in Portuguese) on construction and reduced personal income tax (IRS) on moderate rents (−303 million impact on revenue in 2027), with the rental reform approved in its first reading in September, and with an emergency fund. The street asked for something else in October, in five cities, and the police intervention at the Porto encampment left injured people whose number each source counts its own way. No document in this study says what the 2026 and 2027 measures will do to prices.

What this project concludes

In the two Eurostat indices that compare the twenty-seven, real prices since 2015 and prices against income, Portugal is in first place, and the rise of 16.5 % in the second quarter of 2026 is the largest of the twenty-seven. Rents rise 5.2 % a year in September 2026, against 2.9 % in the Union.

The average hides who pays. Total overburden is lower than in the Union and has fallen since 2015 (from 9.1 % to 6.2 %). Among tenants at market rents the overburden is 27.2 %, 7.5 points above 2020, and they are 16.3 % of the population.

42.1 % of the Portuguese aged twenty-five to thirty-four live with their parents or on the household's income, 12.0 points above the Union. The age of leaving, 28.8 years, is where it was in 2015 (28.9).

In 2025 3.8 dwellings per thousand inhabitants were licensed in Portugal, against 3.6 in the Union; and the 2021 Census counted 723,215 vacant dwellings, 12.1 % of the stock. How many of those are fit to live in, as the Socialist Party (PS) states, no source in this study says.

The housing programme has 2,003 million euros in 2027; the two tax measures have an impact of −303 million on revenue; the rental reform promises more contractual freedom and faster evictions, in the Council of Ministers' own words. None of the documents assesses the effect on prices or rents, and earlier evaluations are not among this study's rows.

The street and the numbers. The injured of the police intervention in Porto are 37 feridos, dois em estado grave according to the movement, 39 feridos, dois deles graves according to the organisation and 35 feridos, pelo menos according to a member of parliament, with um polícia ficou ferido according to the Public Security Police (PSP); the Inspectorate-General of Home Affairs (IGAI) opened a case. This study neither adds nor chooses a count.

What nobody can know from the documents. How many vacant homes are habitable; whether evictions rose (the Left Bloc, BE, says forty percent in 2025; there is no statistical series among this study's sources); how much income each tenth of the population spends on housing; what the 2026 and 2027 measures have done so far.

What could work better

The INE could say, in the Census or in a survey, how many of the vacant dwellings are fit to live in.

A public evaluation of the reduced VAT on construction (how many homes, at what price) before 2029, when the regime ends, would say whether the measure reaches those who need it.

1. What a home costs, and how much it rose

Eurostat's house price index, with 2015 equal to one hundred, stands at 301.43 in Portugal in the second quarter of 2026 and at 168.56 in the Union; Finland stands at 97.50 and Hungary at 389.61, both provisional. In a year, prices rose 16.5 % in Portugal, the largest rise of the twenty-seven, against 4.7 % in the Union and −2.7 % in Finland, the lowest; the INE gives the same 16.5 % for the same quarter. Net of inflation, the 2025 price index stands at 206.41 against 124.75 in the Union, 81.7 points above, with Finland at the lowest (81.90) and Portugal at the highest; in 2020 it stood at 145.91. In 2025 alone, real prices rose 14.7 % in Portugal and 2.9 % in the Union.

Against household income, Eurostat's price-to-income ratio (2015 equal to one hundred) stood at 144.34 in Portugal in 2024 and at 105.72 in the Union, 38.6 points above; Romania has the lowest (55.88) and Portugal the highest. In 2019 Portugal stood at 121.65. Against each country's own long-term average, Portugal stands at 122.92 and the Union at 98.94.

In euros, the INE measures sales: the median price was 2,337 euros per square metre in Q1 2026, 5,292 in Lisbon and 3,729 in Porto; Lisbon costs 2.3 times the country. And the rents of new contracts: 10.17 euros per square metre in Q2 2026, 17.79 in Lisbon, 14.66 in Porto; Lisbon costs 1.7 times the country. By the harmonised index of consumer prices, rents paid rose 5.3 % in 2025 in Portugal against 3.2 % in the Union (Finland 1.0 %, Croatia 17.6 %), and in September 2026 they rise 5.2 % a year against 2.9 %.

2. Who owns, who rents, and how much income goes on the home

In 2025, 71.2 % of the Portuguese lived in their own home (68.5 % in the Union): 31.5 % with a loan (24.8 % in the Union; Romania 1.1 %, the Netherlands 57.9 %) and 39.7 % with no loan (43.7 % in the Union). Tenants were 28.8 % (31.5 % in the Union): 16.3 % at market rents (20.8 % in the Union; Germany 46.8 %) and 12.5 % at reduced or free rent (10.7 %). In 2020, owners with a loan were 38.9 % and tenants at market rents 11.5 %.

Housing absorbs 16.3 % of Portuguese households' disposable income in 2025 (18.9 % in the Union; Cyprus 10.9 %, Greece 34.6 %), less than in 2015 (18.9 %). Overburden, the share of the population spending more than forty percent of its income on housing, is 6.3 % in Portugal and 7.7 % in the Union (Eurostat's dataset by tenure gives 6.2 % for Portugal's total, one tenth below, with the same update date); in 2015 it was 9.1 % in Portugal and 11.2 % in the Union, an estimated value. Cyprus has the lowest (2.4 %) and Greece the highest (26.4 %).

The average hides the rest. Among owners with a loan the overburden is 3.0 % (5.0 % in the Union); among owners with no loan, 1.3 % (3.8 %); among tenants at reduced or free rent, 2.8 % (9.2 %). Among tenants at market rents it is 27.2 %, against 18.6 % in the Union, 8.6 points above; it was 19.7 % in 2020 and 35.4 % in 2015 (Finland has the lowest, 12.3 %, and Romania the highest, 56.0 %). Among the young aged sixteen to twenty-nine, the overburden is 7.5 % (9.3 % in the Union; Greece 25.5 %).

3. The condition of the home

12.7 % of the Portuguese live in overcrowded homes in 2025, against 16.8 % in the Union (Cyprus 2.2 %, Romania 40.4 %); among the young aged sixteen to twenty-nine, 21.2 % (26.6 % in the Union). Severe housing deprivation (overcrowding together with a leaking roof, lack of light, of a bath or of a toilet) affected 6.0 % of the Portuguese in 2023, the last year published, against 4.0 % in the Union (Malta 0.5 %, Latvia 11.6 %), and 9.5 % of the young against 6.4 %. In the condition of the home, Portugal is worse than the Union average; in space, better.

4. The young

The Portuguese leave their parents' home, on average, at 28.8 years, 2.5 years after the Union average (26.3); Finland has the earliest leaving (21.3) and Croatia the latest (31.5). In 2015 it was 28.9. Between twenty-five and thirty-four, 42.1 % live with their parents or depend on the household's income (30.1 % in the Union; Denmark 3.9 %, Croatia 62.3 %); they were 52.3 % in 2020 and 45.7 % in 2015. Between eighteen and thirty-four, 61.0 % against 49.8 %.

5. Supply: what is built and what stands empty

In 2025, 40.9 thousand dwellings were licensed in Portugal (a provisional value), against 1,623.2 thousand in the Union: per thousand inhabitants, 3.8 in Portugal and 3.6 in the Union. The stock counted by the 2021 Census has 5,970,677 dwellings, of which 723,215 were vacant, 12.1 %; the INE splits them into «Vago para venda ou arrendamento», 348,097, and «Vago por outros motivos», 375,118. The PS speaks of «centenas de milhares de casas vazias em condições de serem habitadas. Para as trazer para o mercado, o PS vai propor um regime excecional, válido durante três anos:»; the habitability condition has no source and no period in this study's documents.

6. Credit

In August 2026 the banks lent 2,553.0 million euros in new housing loans, at a rate of 3.0 %, according to the Bank of Portugal; the stock of households' housing credit was 119,061.6 million. The implicit interest rate on all contracts, by the INE, was 3.162 % in August 2026.

7. What the State does: the money and the laws

The 2027 Budget gives the housing programme 2,003 million euros: 1,071 million in the action «Ação 1.1 Promoção da oferta e reabilitação habitacional», 917 million in the action «Ação 1.3 Promoção do acesso à habitação e apoio ao arrendamento», 15 million in the action «Ação 1.2 Gestão urbanística e qualidade do edificiado».

The report says of the support for the young: «Para os jovens, manter-se-á a garantia pública na compra da primeira habitação, bem como as isenções de IMT e de IS na aquisição da primeira habitação própria e permanente, que já beneficiaram quase 120 mil jovens, para um valor médio de aquisição de imóvel de 200 mil euros. Adicionalmente, o Orçamento do Estado reforçará o programa Porta 65, através do qual o Estado apoia os jovens no arrendamento para habitação permanente.» Of the tax measures: «Como o desafio não se circunscreve apenas a esta faixa etária, na frente fiscal, o Governo aprovou ainda a redução do IRS para 10% nas rendas até 2300 euros, o aumento da dedução à coleta de IRS relativa às despesas com o arrendamento e a redução do imposto sobre valor o acrescentado (IVA) da construção para 6%, como estímulo ao aumento da oferta num setor gravemente afetado pela crise económico-financeira de 2008-2014.» Of supply: «Do lado da oferta pública, o Governo já concluiu mais de 30 mil casas, das quais cerca de mil no regime de arrendamento acessível. Para 2027, a prioridade será a mobilização de investimento privado, através de parcerias público-privadas com imóveis do Estado, e a execução do programa de financiamento das 45 mil habitações excluídas do PRR.»

The two tax measures with an impact in 2027 come from the decree-law of May 2026: «Habitação - redução IVA construção de 23% para 6% (i)», −94 million euros of impact on revenue in 2027, and «Habitação - redução IRS rendas moderadas de 25% para 10% (i)», −209 million; −303 million together. The Council of Ministers announced them thus in September 2025: «Redução da taxa de IVA para 6% na construção de habitações para venda até 648 mil euros ou, no caso de arrendamento, para rendas até 2.300 euros. Este regime fiscal vigora até 2029.»

The decree-law sets the rate on rents: «1 — A taxa de tributação autónoma aplicável aos rendimentos prediais decorrentes de contratos de arrendamento destinados exclusivamente ao arrendamento para habitação cujo valor de renda mensal não exceda os limites a que se referem os n.os 2 e 3 do artigo 2.º do Decreto-Lei n.º 97/2026, de 20 de maio, auferidos até 31 de dezembro de 2029, é de 10 %, salvo quando seja aplicável uma taxa mais favorável.» And the rent deduction in the income tax: «1 — O limite previsto no n.º 10 do artigo 78.º-E do Código do IRS, na redação conferida pelo presente decreto-lei, aplica-se a partir do ano de 2027, inclusive, aplicando-se no ano de 2026 o limite de 900,00 €.» For 2027, the limit is «10 — O limite a que se refere a alínea a) do n.º 1 é elevado para 1000,00 €, sem prejuízo da aplicação do n.º 4, quando o limite de dedução daí resultante seja superior.». The PS proposes «Arrendatários: dedução das rendas no IRS aumentada para 1.000 euros a partir de 2027.»

The rental reform: the Council of Ministers of July 2026 described it thus: «A reforma assenta em quatro eixos principais: maior liberdade contratual, reforço da segurança e do cumprimento dos contratos, simplificação e aceleração dos mecanismos de despejo e resolução de litígios, e reforço da responsabilidade social do Estado na proteção das situações de maior vulnerabilidade.» And announced the Housing Emergency Fund (FEH): «2. Aprovou um Decreto-Lei que cria o Fundo de Emergência para a Habitação (FEH) destinado a apoiar pessoas e famílias em situação de emergência habitacional, tais como despejos motivados por carência económica ou situações de violência doméstica. O FEH introduz um apoio financeiro direto, não reembolsável, para garantir um realojamento rápido, temporário ou permanente, permitindo que ninguém fique sem solução imediata em caso de perda de casa. O FEH visa assegurar que é o Estado, e não os proprietários, a assumir os custos sociais das situações de emergência habitacional.» The enabling bill says, in its explanatory memorandum: «Nomeadamente, propõe-se que a resolução do contrato possa ocorrer quando haja mora igual ou superior a dois meses no pagamento da renda ou quando exista atraso superior a oito dias no pagamento de rendas, por mais de três vezes, seguidas ou interpoladas, num período de 12 meses, ou por mais de quatro vezes num período de 18 meses.» It was approved in its first reading on 30 de setembro 2026. The Social Democratic Party (PSD), Liberal Initiative (IL) and the Democratic and Social Centre, People’s Party (CDS-PP) voted in favour.
