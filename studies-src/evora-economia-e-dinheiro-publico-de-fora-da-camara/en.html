The economy of Évora and the public money that reaches the municipality outside the council

In brief

What does the municipality of Évora produce, what public money reaches it outside the council, through whose hands, and how much of it is late? This study brings together the municipality's statistics (the accounts of its enterprises, foreign trade, tourism, the population and work series), the large investments on record, the recovery-plan money attributed to the municipality, public contracts, the Tribunal de Contas audit catalogue and the funding doors open in August 2026.

Évora is a relatively prosperous municipality in a region below the national average: the 2023 purchasing-power index puts the municipality at 111.5 and its region at 93.9, with the national average at one hundred. The 7 907 enterprises based in the municipality generated €576 491 544 of value added in 2024 (what they produced minus what they bought in order to produce). The recovery plan attributes €167 372 756 to the municipality, of which €86 944 669 was paid in the snapshot of 2026-08-19, and most of it goes through bodies that are not the council.

What this project concludes

The outside money does not go through the council. Of the recovery-plan money contracted in the municipality, the Universidade de Évora holds €38 596 976 and the municipality €12 069 013, and those who administer it are national agencies. Questions about this total go more often to the national bodies and to the university than to the town hall.

The delay shows in the money, and the dates do not disprove it. The overdue project locations carry 61.32% of the value approved for the municipality, and the completion dates that would soften that reading are the frailest field in the register: 1 011 of the 1 445 on-time locations end exactly on their planned date. Two large promises stand still with nothing paid: the Malagueira student residence and the heavy medical equipment for the new central hospital of the Alentejo.

The economy is concentrated, and each person employed by the municipality's enterprises generates less value added than in the country. Four enterprises hold 21.5% of the municipality's enterprise value added, against 2.6% in the country; value added per person employed is €23 004, against €31 900 in the country; and the aeronautics cluster exports far more than it generates in the municipality. The purchasing-power index puts the municipality above the national average and value added per person employed puts it below, but they are different measures of different populations (those who live in the municipality, in one case, and those who work for the enterprises based there, in the other), and they do not say how much the municipality consumes.

The town fared well on the national indices, which moved with the country's tides and not with a council's. Registered unemployment at the end of December fell from 3 720 in 2013 to 1 596 in 2024, and tourist nights went from 312 696 in 2011 to 709 328 in 2024. The series that ends below where it started is purchasing power, which went from 118.65 in 2009 to 111.47 in 2023.

The searchable audit trail is thinnest where the money is thickest: in the four families of the audit catalogue that were read, nothing published after 2019 names Évora in its title or abstract, and those are the years in which the recovery-plan money moved.

What the documents do not allow anyone to know: a gross domestic product for the town, which does not exist; a European Union figure per municipality, which does not exist either; and what the Tribunal de Contas concluded about Évora's money, because the catalogue indexes abstracts and not the reports.

What could work better

The public registers being joined by the tax number of the entities, which the recovery-plan register already carries in its rows and which the contracts search and the audit catalogue do not return. The recovery-plan register keeping the history of its daily snapshots. And that register's completion dates saying whether they were observed or filled in by convention.

The limits of this document

  • There is no GDP figure for the city. National accounts stop at NUTS3 — Alentejo Central, 14 municipalities of which Évora is one. What exists at concelho level is the business register: the accounts of enterprises headquartered in the concelho (INE's SCIE). Enterprise GVA is not municipal GDP: it misses public administration, most of the university and the hospital, and it credits a firm's whole activity to its head-office concelho.
  • A call counts as reachable when its programme is the Alentejo regional programme or a national one. National avisos can still restrict territory inside the PDF; the count is therefore an upper bound on what an Évora actor can apply to. Who-can-apply classes come from the eligibility sections of the Fund Access Register, of 2026-08-08.
  • Investment values are contract values, not audited outturn. The Embraer and Mecachrome figures are what the State's own resolutions and the institutions' pages print — what was committed, not a measure of what the plants are worth today.
  • Several dates coexist. Business structure is 2024 (INE's most recent SCIE year), trade and tourism reach 2025, the municipality's average monthly earnings reach 2024 in the table of series and the comparison with the national average stays at 2022, and funding doors are 2026-08-10.
  • The section on the unexplored room is inference. Everything before it is a sourced fact.
  • A snapshot. Calls close continuously — three of the doors listed were already inside their final month on 2026-08-10.

The Tribunal de Contas catalogue read here covers 4 of the Tribunal's product families rather than all of them.

The contracts section is an upper bound on a truncated window. Portal BASE holds 24 436 contracts for the concelho of Évora. 600 of them are the most recently published ones, spanning 2026-05-08 to 2026-08-04. A contract carried out across several municipalities is returned by each of their filters at its full value, so a concelho contract total is an upper bound and concelho totals must never be added together.

There is no EU figure for a municipality. The finest geography the EU's project-level data reaches for Portugal is the NUTS3 region. That is a null.

The frame: a city above its region

58 567 people lived in the concelho in 2025 (up from 55 711 in 2021). The surrounding NUTS3 region, Alentejo Central, produced a GDP of €3554.5 million in 2024 (provisional), which is 1.2% of the country's output — €23 164 per head against €27 063 nationally, or 85.6 on a Portugal-equals-100 scale, and 70.5 against the EU-27 average in purchasing-power terms.

The city itself sits above the national average on the one indicator that exists at concelho level: INE's 2023 purchasing-power index puts Évora at 111.5 (Portugal = 100), while its own region stands at 93.9. Évora is a relatively prosperous city inside a poor region.

The population series steps up between 2020 and 2021 and keeps climbing after it. Municipal population estimates before 2011 exist only on a superseded geographical base and are not comparable, which is why 2009 and 2010 are empty.

The 7 907 enterprises headquartered in the concelho employed 25 061 people in 2024, turned over €1 833 280 534, and generated €576 491 544 of gross value added — 10.5% above 2023 in nominal terms (2023: €521 625 136).

Manufacturing is the largest single block of enterprise value added in the concelho. Within it, CAE division 30 — fabrication of other transport equipment, the aeronautics plants' own division — shows €24 573 447 of GVA and 914 employed in 2024. Limit 1: SCIE attributes by company seat and by the firm's principal activity, and a plant-level picture is exactly what a municipality-level business register cannot promise.

Concentration is the structural fact. The four largest enterprises hold 21.5% of all enterprise GVA in the concelho, 23.4% of turnover and 21.0% of employment — the same four-firm GVA measure for Portugal as a whole is 2.6%. The size structure behind it: 7 605 micro-enterprises, 255 small, 39 medium and 8 large firms. In 2025 the concelho registered 259 new incorporations against 67 dissolutions (236 and 79 in 2024).

Tourism: recovered past 2019, and about to be tested

739 187 nights were spent in the concelho's tourist accommodation in 2025 — 12.1% above the pre-pandemic 2019 figure of 659 561 — by 440 694 guests, 46.1% of them non-resident. Total accommodation revenue was €53 779 000 in 2025 (2023: €47 593 000). Tourism intensity, at 1261.1 nights per hundred residents, is well above the national 719 — but the stay is short: 1.7 nights per guest on average. People come, and they pass through. The European Capital of Culture year, 2027, is the stress test ahead.

Who has put serious money in

Aeronautics. The cluster began as a State decision: on 23 September 2008 the Portuguese State signed two investment contracts with Embraer for two plants in Évora — €117 143 105 for metallic structures and €52 281 430 for composites, €169 424 535 combined, the values printed in Resolução do Conselho de Ministros n.º 69/2010. In May 2022 Aernnova concluded the acquisition of both plants, which its own announcement puts at 500 employees. Mecachrome followed the anchor: a State investment contract signed on 3 February 2016 for a plant of around €30 million producing titanium aircraft-engine parts, financed in part by a €40 million European Investment Bank loan under the Investment Plan for Europe. The 2014-2020 EU cycle backed all three plants' expansion: €11 847 881 of ERDF money for the metallic plant's DESIAM project, €11 125 982 for the composites plant's DESi2COMP, and €10 924 905 for Mecachrome's production unit (EU register values, located to NUTS3, not concelho).

Health. The new central hospital — the Hospital Central do Alentejo — drew €40 000 000 of ERDF money for its outpatient block in the 2014-2020 cycle (EU register, NUTS3). The heavy medical equipment for the same hospital has approved recovery-plan money and, in the snapshot of 2026-08-19, nothing paid, as the section on the recovery plan shows.

Knowledge. The Universidade de Évora reports 9 106 students and 311 research projects in execution on its own numbers page; the PACT science and technology park carried an ERDF project of €4 355 421 in the 2014-2020 cycle. Under the PRR, the New Space Portugal mobilising agenda has €9 792 916 of its approved money attributed to Évora locations.

Culture. The money of the 2027 European Capital of Culture is in its own study, with the documents each value comes from.

The municipality. The Município de Évora's 2026 budget, approved on 22 January 2026, is in the study The accounts of the Câmara de Évora, 2010 to 2025. The industrial land offer, PITE, spans 500 000 m² gross; the municipal pages publish its lot structure but no occupancy rate.

No Council of Ministers resolution 2023-2026 approves a new contractual-regime investment located in Évora — the recent AICEP contract batches name Sines, Estarreja, Boticas, Portalegre and Rio Maior, not Évora. A widely reported Lauak plant for Évora is traceable to no primary source and its Portuguese plants sit elsewhere; a reported green hydrogen project and a light-aircraft factory remain press-only. The last State-contracted industrial anchor is a decade old.

Status stands at the snapshot's own reference date, not today's, because that is the date the source speaks for.

1 011 of the 1 445 locations counted as on time record an actual completion date falling exactly on the planned date — 69.97% of them. A modal difference of zero at that scale is more consistent with a field being closed out administratively than with delivery being observed and dated (inference). The remaining 434 locations record an actual date earlier than the planned one, and the same doubt about how these fields are filled applies to them too.

Two things sharpen that reading, and one of them stops it being a criticism of Évora. First, 470 of those exact matches are rows where the start date, the planned completion date and the actual completion date are all the same day. A project cannot be observed to begin and finish on one date at that scale, so those rows record a filing convention rather than an observed date (inference). All of them sit in a single investment line. Second, the pattern is national, not local: across the country 177 241 of 246 545 on-time locations carry the same exact-date stamp, 71.89%, which is slightly higher than Évora's. The register's completion dates cannot be read as observed delivery anywhere. So the on-time count is soft evidence, and quoting it bare would overstate punctuality by a wide margin.

The overdue group is the one that reads as a broken promise: a planned completion date already passed at the snapshot date, with no actual completion date recorded. In Évora that is 608 project-locations carrying €102 624 704 of approved money — 61.32% of everything approved for the concelho — of which €41 693 864 has been paid.

It is concentrated. Habitação has 39 project-locations in Évora, all of them past their planned date with nothing recorded as delivered: every one of them overdue. Two of the five largest project-locations in the concelho are in that state and have received nothing at all — the student residence at €7 483 162 approved and €0 paid, and the hospital equipment purchase at €5 314 868 approved and €0 paid.

The equipment is for the new central hospital of the Alentejo; the money is approved, the planned date has passed, and nothing has been disbursed (inference: the source records approval, date and payment, and does not say why).

The grouping rule is not the register's: fold accents, drop punctuation, drop the trailing legal-form abbreviation. The rule under-merges — the intermunicipal community in the table above files under its acronym and, twice, under the same name without it, which the rule does not join because the acronym survives normalisation. Together those are 14 contracts worth €735 681. Nothing available can distinguish an acronym prefix from a genuinely different body. So 5 is a floor on how many bodies file under more than one name, not a count. That is the shape of this whole register: a per-string count looks like a per-entity count and is not one.

Across the two sources, the same institutions recur in a different order, and one of them is easy to lose. The hospital is the largest contracting entity in the window above; in the recovery-plan data it appears under a different name, HOSPITAL DO ESPÍRITO SANTO DE ÉVORA, E.P.E., with €7 746 863 contracted and €563 527 paid. A join on entity names across the two registers would have missed it (inference — the two registers use different names for what the contract objects themselves confirm is one body; nothing in either source states that they are the same entity).

Why a silence here is not a silence

Two blind spots.

The four families are not the Tribunal's output. They are four of the product families it publishes. A fifth — Relatórios de Apuramento de Responsabilidades, 192 documents — contains 2 reports whose subject is the Município de Évora itself:

  • Relatório nº 1/2016 - 1ª Secção — «Auditoria ao Município de Évora para o apuramento de responsabilidade financeira sancionatória identificada no âmbito do contrato de abertura de crédito na modalidade de “curto prazo” outorgado com o Millennium BCP»
  • Relatório nº 8/2013 - ARF — «Contrato de empreitada “Conceção/Construção da Escola EB1/JI dos Canaviais” celebrado entre o Município de Évora e o Consórcio “Ecociaf – Construção Civil e Obras Públicas, Lda. e Certar – Sociedade de Construções, S.A.” (Processo de fiscalização Prévia nº 1968/2009)»

So the true count of catalogued Tribunal documents about this municipality is at least three, not one, and the four-family search cannot see two of them.

The catalogue indexes abstracts, not report bodies. A thematic audit of how mainland municipalities fund their fire brigades carries no mention of Évora in its catalogue entry at all — and names the concelho 13 times in its own text, including its position in the Tribunal's own tables.

In the four families, nothing published after 2019 names Évora in its title or abstract.

Évora is an unusually clean name to search for: 0 structural risks against it, that is, no structural over-match risk. It is not one of the two Portuguese municipality names that belong to two places at once, and it is not a whole word inside a longer municipality's name. The gazetteer is INE's own live list of 308 municipalities. Names that fail those tests behave very differently — a search for "Guarda" in this catalogue returns the national guard, not the city.

What the EU data cannot tell you

There is no EU figure for the concelho of Évora. The granularity does not exist in the source.

The EU's project-level dataset places 2 569 projects in the NUTS3 region containing Évora, which it names Alentejo Central. All 2 569 of them carry the programming period 2014-2020 — an earlier funding cycle, and a different instrument from the recovery plan. Putting the two side by side would be comparing different things. The dataset has no concelho field, and for Portugal the postal code is empty on every row.

The size of the aggregation problem is the point. Alentejo Central contains 14 municipalities, per INE's own geographic dimension: Alandroal, Arraiolos, Borba, Estremoz, Montemor-o-Novo, Mora, Mourão, Portel, Redondo, Reguengos de Monsaraz, Vendas Novas, Viana do Alentejo, Vila Viçosa, Évora. A figure for that region is a figure for all of them.

PRR publishes the older NUTS vintage — the same dimension carries 25 NUTS3 entries — while the EU project data uses a newer one. Both call the region Alentejo Central; the codes are not interchangeable, and passing one to the other returns zero rows with a perfectly healthy HTTP status.

Where the unexplored room is

The funding doors this section quotes are those of the annex, read on 2026-08-10; the deadlines up to 2026-09-09 have passed, as the annex says.

1. The aerospace cluster exports far more than it feeds locally. Chapter 88 alone exports €199 951 040, yet division 30 shows €24 573 447 of GVA and 914 jobs, and four firms hold 21.5% of all enterprise GVA. The gap between a world-scale export figure and a modest local value-added figure is what a thin supplier base looks like: machining, treatment, tooling, logistics and certification services around three anchor plants are the classic entry points, and the funding geometry fits — BPF guarantee lines are standing, the PT2030 company calls are open, and ESA BIC incubation exists precisely for that adjacency. The last State-contracted anchor is a decade old; the next tier was never contracted.

2. Tourism's constraint is the short stay, and the Capital of Culture year will test capacity it hasn't built. Intensity already runs well above the national average, yet the average guest stays 1.7 nights and total accommodation revenue is €53 779 000 — a pass-through profile, with 2027 about to multiply demand onto it. The doors are open now — Qualification of Supply to €3 000 000 per project, Crescer com o Turismo until end-2026 — and close before the demand peak arrives. Capacity and stay-lengthening investment made in 2026 meets the 2027 test; investment decided in 2027 does not.

3. Nobody has consolidated Évora 2027's money, which usually means nobody has consolidated its procurement either. The official pieces on record do not add to a programme budget (the study Évora 2027, European Capital of Culture sets them side by side, document by document), and the association's contracts flow through Portal BASE. For local firms (staging, production, accessibility works, hospitality), the ECoC's supply needs are a 2026-2027 revenue stream that the published doors barely describe. The DGArtes orchestra call (€1 620 000, deadline 2026-09-09) is the concrete, dated example.

4. The countryside's door is not where its money is. Agriculture generates €45 228 740 of enterprise GVA and €65 103 634 of live-animal exports, but PEPAC's open windows are forestry-only and Portugal 2030 structurally excludes farmers. On 2026-08-10, the windows open to an Évora landowner were the six forestry and ecosystem calls with deadlines between August and September 2026, and those deadlines have passed; it was money for the montado rather than for the herd. Anyone waiting for the next productive-investment or young-farmers window is waiting on IFAP's calendar, not on Portugal 2030's.

5. The city's own execution is the quiet constraint. €102 624 704 of PRR money attributed to Évora sits, in the snapshot of 2026-08-19, in overdue locations while the construction section already generates €49 641 927 of GVA with 2 106 workers. Deadlines (PRR closes 2026) turn unbuilt approvals into forfeited money; execution capacity — project management, licensed contractors, works supervision — is itself a market in Évora for exactly as long as the backlog exists.

6. The productivity gap is the compound finding. GVA per worker of €23 004 against €31 900 nationally, wages €1 282 against €1 362 (2022), a micro-enterprise base of 7 605 firms — and, at the same time, an above-average purchasing-power index of 111.5.
