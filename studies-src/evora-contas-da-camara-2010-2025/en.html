The Câmara de Évora paid its loans on time; the average time to pay suppliers reached 137 days

The accounts of the Câmara de Évora, 2010 to 2025: the budget, what was collected, the debt and the certification

Are the accounts of the Câmara de Évora in order?

It keeps within the legal debt limit; it pays those who sell to it late. Debt closed 2025 at €54 379 035, with €28 192 653 of headroom under the legal limit, but at the end of the year €14.6 million of invoices were waiting to be paid. Loan repayments kept to the repayment plans, and staff spending came to 96.55% of the budget.

The average time to pay suppliers was 137 days in 2025; in 2021 it was 49. The delay has a price: the 2025 accounts record €622 495 of default interest, more than three times the €190 000 the initial budget provided for. Among those who bill it are the water and waste companies.

What we conclude

  • Unpaid invoices more than doubled in two years. At the end of 2025, €14.6 million of invoices were waiting to be paid; at the end of 2023 the figure was about 5.9 million.
  • The legal debt almost stopped falling. In 2025 it fell by €301 601. The principal of medium and long-term loans went down by €3 549 470 and the debt in the supplier headings went up by €3 674 532; these headings include the water debt assigned to the European Investment Bank.
  • Almost half of what the budget fell short by sits in a single line. The line «outras receitas correntes» (other current revenue) budgeted €19 427 778 and collected €155 573; the report says the line results from the need to accommodate debt carried over. The rest of the shortfall is mostly transfers from the State and from co-financed projects that did not arrive.
  • Under the municipal heading for Évora 2027, almost nothing was paid in 2025. The plan gave that heading €3 796 940 for the year, and €55 594 was paid; the five projects paid for by the recovery plan record no payments in 2025.
  • The year without certification has a documented explanation, and the matter remains unresolved. The annex to the 2025 accounts says the 2024 accounts were not approved because the differences between the accounting records and the bank records could not be validated. Excluding the items resolved in 2026, the annex calculates a net unreconciled balance of €195 040 at the end of 2025, resulting from differences of €770 120 in one direction and €575 080 in the other. It reports an improvement but gives no figure for previous years. The same annex says that court proceedings are under way concerning «uma situação de alcance na Tesouraria» (a shortfall in the Treasury), and that the unexplained entries identified in the audit of 2022 and 2023 remain unresolved.

What is going well, what is going badly

Going well.

  • The debt index, in the series of the regulator (the Direção-Geral das Autarquias Locais), fell from 242.6% in 2014 to 105.5% in 2024, and the debt has been under the legal limit since 2020.
  • Loans were repaid at 93.64% of the amount budgeted, with every instalment paid.
  • The sale of goods and services and income from property collected more than the budget provided for.
  • The auditor certified the 2025 accounts with an unmodified opinion, with four emphasis-of-matter paragraphs.
  • The 2026 budget lowers the line «outras receitas correntes» to €7 615 243.

Going badly.

  • Arrears are back: €4 976 172 at the end of 2025, when 2023 had been the eighth year in a row with none.
  • In the plan's three lines for water and sanitation (Águas do Vale do Tejo) and for waste (Gesamb), €7 560 873 was paid of the €12 320 000 provided for.
  • The net result for 2025 was a loss of €2 443 292; in 2024 it had been a loss of €4 591 039.
  • Payments for investment amounted to 21.13% of the budgeted sum, although they rose by 7.4% compared with 2024; the report attributes part of the gap to tenders that attracted no bids and recovery-plan funding still to be received.
  • The 2026 budget provides for €110 840 577; in 2025 executed revenue was €67 263 297, including €1 997 375 carried over from 2024.
  • The search records, compiled on 10 August 2026, say the 2024 accounts were not found on the municipal pages consulted.

For whom

For those who sell to the council. An average of 137 days is four and a half months of waiting. At the end of the year, the schedule of debts recorded €4 976 172 overdue by 90 days or more, of which €357 328 by more than a year.

For those who pay for water and waste collection. The 2026 budget provides for €14 476 737 from the sale of goods and services, which is where water, sanitation and waste sit: €6 153 637 more than the 2025 budget. The budget report does not explain the increase. The documents read do not say whether tariffs go up; that is to be read in the 2026 tariff table and in the resolution that approves it.

For local clubs and associations. The report says that support planned for sports associations had not been committed by the end of the year. The accounts read do not show how much was left unallocated to each club.

For those waiting for a public work. Of the €24 159 745 of investment budgeted in 2025, €5 104 307 was paid. In 2021, €7 537 452 had been paid.

Our reading

The Câmara de Évora keeps within the legal debt limit and pays those who sell to it late.

Our reading is that the council pays first what cannot wait, salaries and loans, and finances the rest with its suppliers' time and with postponed works. The accounts show both movements in the same year: €3 549 470 less in loan principal and €3 674 532 more in the supplier headings.

What is going well is a decade of decline in the debt index, which the regulator's series confirms. What is going badly is that the debt in euros almost stopped falling in 2025, and the report does not say so up front. In 2025 total debt fell by €301 601; from 2021 to 2023, in the same reports, it fell by more than three million a year, before the debt came to include, in 2024, the energy-efficiency contract.

For those who live in Évora, the budget has lost value as a forecast: in 2025 the budget, after revisions, was €109 483 315, and executed revenue, including the balance carried over, was €67 263 297. And the five Évora 2027 projects paid for by the recovery plan reached the end of 2025 without payments and have an allocation again in 2026.

The recommendations

  • To the Câmara Municipal de Évora: publish a schedule for paying the €4 976 172 in arrears, quarter by quarter, before the legal deadline for the 2026 accounts, at the end of April 2027. While debts remain in default, interest can keep adding up. The reader can follow it in the average payment time and the arrears in the 2026 accounts, and in the schedule itself, if it is published with what was paid in each quarter.
  • To the Câmara and the Assembleia Municipal: in the 2027 budget, write next to the line «outras receitas correntes» how much carried-over debt it accommodates and to whom it is owed. Without that explanation, the revenue execution rate remains hard to read. The reader can check it in the 2027 budget report.
  • To the Câmara Municipal de Évora: say by 31 December 2026, the end date of the four Évora 2027 construction contracts in the plan, how much has been paid on each. Said by that date, those figures are known before the year of the European Capital of Culture begins. The reader can check it in the statement of execution of the 2026 plan.

What to watch

  • 31 December 2026: the end of the four Évora 2027 construction contracts in the plan, and the completion of the bank reconciliations, which the auditor expects in 2026.
  • By the end of April 2027: the legal deadline for the 2026 accounts; in them, the average payment time, the arrears and what the line «outras receitas correntes» collected.
  • In the same accounts: what was paid on the Rossio de São Brás, which carries €9 500 000† in 2026, of which €7 150 000† without defined financing.
  • The Évora 2027 association: the line bearing its name provided €250 000 for 2025, within a total of €8 250 000 over several years, and had no payments that year; in the 2026 plan it carries €1 000†.
  • The regulator's file for 2025: at the reading of 10 August 2026, the latest was the one for 2024.

The debt: suppliers, banks and the legal total

The debt recorded under the supplier headings closed 2025 at €19 821 418, up €3 674 532 in a year, 22.76%. Banks: the medium and long-term loans

Medium and long-term bank debt ended the year at €29 418 657, down €3 549 470 on the year, across seven loans and three banks. The loans that replaced the PAEL debt, the Programa de Apoio à Economia Local (Local Economy Support Programme), are 49% of the principal outstanding; the three financial-recovery (saneamento financeiro) loans contracted in 2016 are 42%. Together they are about 91% of the bank debt; the investment loan contracted in 2023 is about 6%. Servicing all of it cost €5 365 896 in 2025: €4 308 783 of principal and €1 057 113 of interest.

The total, against the legal ceiling

The legal measure is total debt of budgetary operations under the 2013 local finance law. It ended 2025 at €54 379 035: €54 261 280 the municipality's own, €117 754 its share of the participated entities. The law caps that total at one and a half times the average net current revenue of the previous three years. For Évora, that is a ceiling of €82 571 687 on an average of €55 047 791, so 2025 closes in compliance, with €28 192 653 of headroom.

What the auditor signed, and what could not be signed

The Certificação Legal das Contas de 2025, signed 2026-04-17 by Marques, Cruz & Associados, SROC, gives an unmodified opinion: the financial statements give a true and fair view of the municipality's financial position, and the budgetary statements comply with public-sector accounting standards. It contains four emphasis-of-matter paragraphs, which highlight matters without modifying the opinion. The first concerns work to resolve bank reconciliation discrepancies, begun in 2025 and expected to finish in 2026. The second concerns the derecognition of the assets and liabilities of the low-voltage electricity-grid concession under accounting guidance issued in 2025. The other two concern about 2.8 million euros in non-current transfer payables and the existence of arrears.

The proposed Prestação de Contas de 2024 was rejected at the Câmara meeting of 2025-05-28, with 2 votes in favour and 5 against. The auditor therefore issued a Declaração de Impossibilidade de Certificação Legal das Contas for 2024 (a declaration that statutory certification of the accounts was not possible). The 2024 accounts were left without legal certification. The opinion on 2025 does not certify 2024 retroactively.

The annex to the 2025 accounts gives the reason: the 2024 accounts were not approved because the differences found in the bank reconciliation could not be fully validated. The annex reports a net unreconciled balance of €195 040 at the end of 2025 after excluding the entries resolved in early 2026, and mentions ongoing court proceedings concerning a shortfall in the Treasury. The documents read do not explain why each member voted as they did.

How to read the tables and the series

The national debt table in the presentation of the Anuário Financeiro dos Municípios Portugueses 2024 ends at accounts year 2023; DGAL's per-municipality file reaches 2024. The Anuário is an academic publication (CICF/IPCA, with the ordem dos contabilistas, the accountants' professional body); its national table uses DGAL data. According to the record of the collection of 10 August 2026, only the conclusions presentation was found openly published.

"Debt" does not mean the same thing in every year of the table. Until 2013, the legal regime used endividamento líquido municipal (net municipal debt). With the 2013 local finance law, in force since 1 January 2014, the measure became dívida total de operações orçamentais (total debt of budgetary operations), which includes the municipality's share of the entities it participates in. The municipality also publishes a third figure, dívida global apurada (global debt as assessed), and a fourth, dívida orçamental (budgetary debt) without the participated entities. For several years two or three of these coexist in the same report with different values.

The accounting system changed underneath the series. The municipality kept its accounts under POCAL for the first part of the period and under SNC-AP for the later part. The 2021 report says in its own words that the new system makes results look worse than the old one, because of how it treats depreciation and amortisation. Results before and after the transition are therefore not one series, and the result column of the finance table should be read as two.

At the collection of 10 August 2026, no text was obtained from the scanned 2020 dossier, by direct extraction and without character recognition, nor were the 2024 accounts found on the municipal pages consulted. The 2020 and 2024 figures from the municipality's accounts therefore come from later reports: from their comparative columns or their text.

An asterisk in the tables means the figure comes from a later report's comparative column, not from a document of its own year. It is the same municipality reporting, but in a later report, sometimes written by another executive. For 2024, that year's own accounts were rejected. Five years carry it in part or in whole.

A dagger means the figure comes from a degraded scan. It appears only on figures from the 2026 plan. It is the same kind of marker as the asterisk. The plan's report body extracts cleanly; its annexes, the plano plurianual de investimentos (multi-year investment plan) and the plano de atividades (activity plan), where the project lines live, are a scanned layer in which digits and designations are frequently unreadable. Every figure taken from that layer carries a dagger. Lines that could not be recovered were left out; they are gaps, not zeros.

The finance table, 2010 to 2025

The mandate boundaries are marked in the table, but the figures are annual: 2013, 2017, 2021 and 2025 each have two executives, and the table does not split the year between them. Values marked with an asterisk come from a later report's comparative column. A dash means the table does not have the figure, not that it is zero. Results are shown as losses unless preceded by a plus sign.

The revenue column is not one concept across the whole period. For 2010 to 2014, it shows budgetary revenue received as reported in the cash-flow statement; from 2015, it shows the total in the budget-execution table, which in most of those years explicitly includes the closing balance carried over from the previous year. When the revenue execution total includes the balance from the previous year, it exceeds the year's own collection by the amount of that balance.

Three of the years in the revenue column include large loans. The 2013 figures carry the support-programme instalments, the 2016 figures the financial-recovery loan, the 2019 figures the replacement loan. In each of those three years, the revenue column rises sharply and falls back the following year.

The result column is two series, not one, because of the POCAL to SNC-AP transition, and only one positive year appears in it.

In the extraction of 10 August 2026, no usable text was obtained from the management reports for 2010 to 2014; no character recognition was done. For the first years, the cash-flow and income statements published separately were read; 2010 and 2011 have, in the finance table, three columns filled in and four left blank.

One number in the table needs its footnote printed beside it. The 2016 result of minus €3 337 288 includes, as a cost of 2016, VAT relating to the years 2009 to 2011: €1 712 963. The report writes the subtraction of the two and states as the corrected figure a loss of €1 984 259, but the subtraction it prints does not give that figure. Both results are the municipality's own: the table uses the first, the net result for the year, because it is what the accounts record and the same measure as the other years; the corrected one stays as the report writes it, with no arithmetic that explains it.

Two of the years of the 2017 to 2021 mandate are the pandemic years, and the 2020 figures in the finance table all carry the comparative asterisk because the published 2020 dossier has no text.

The 2025 accounts straddle the handover: they cover a year in which the executive elected in 2021 governed until 2025-10-31 and the next one from that date. They are printed in the finance table as a single year, because that is how they exist, and they are attributed to neither mandate.
