Read this part first

Three limits govern everything that follows. They are here rather than in a footnote because each one is the difference between a true sentence and a false one.

The audit section reads the Tribunal's catalogue, not its audits. What was fetched is the title and abstract of each published document. That is enough to say what was audited, when, and where to read it. It is not enough to say what the Tribunal concluded — the findings, the recommendations and the euro amounts are inside the PDFs, and nothing here opens one. Any sentence in this document that reads like a verdict on Évora's management would be an invention. There are none, and that is deliberate. It also reads 4 of the Tribunal's product families rather than all of them, which turns out to matter enough that the section measures the cost instead of mentioning it.

The contracts section is an upper bound on a truncated window. Portal BASE holds 24 436 contracts for the concelho of Évora. This document read 600 of them — the most recently published ones, spanning 2026-05-08 to 2026-08-04. The rest were not read. A contract carried out across several municipalities is returned by each of their filters at its full value, so a concelho contract total is an upper bound and concelho totals must never be added together.

There is no EU figure for a municipality, and this document does not manufacture one. The finest geography the EU's project-level data reaches for Portugal is the NUTS3 region. That is a null, established by probe rather than asserted, and it is set out at the end.

One thing this document can do that a single source cannot: it puts the promise, the payment and the audit record of one place side by side. Where it reasons beyond what a source states, the sentence says (inference).

What was promised, and what was paid

The Plano de Recuperação e Resiliência publishes a full-refresh snapshot every week, with no version history: last week's file is simply gone from the source. The snapshot read here carries the reference date 2026-08-03.

Both figures are sourced facts, not estimates. They come from the manager layer of the beneficiaries file — the Beneficiário Intermediário and Beneficiário Direto rows — which gives exactly one row per project-location and carries both the approved and the paid amount for that location. The count of those rows, 397 634, equals the row count of the geographic distribution file exactly, which is the structural check that the layer decomposes the money once and only once.

An independent route to the same approved figure — rebuilding it from the percentage share column in the locations file — lands €1 169 away, which is four-decimal rounding in the published share and nothing else. The other share column in that same file, the one that looks like a per-location payment share, is not used: it repeats the project-wide payment ratio on every row, and reading it as a share inflates Évora's paid figure by an order of magnitude. That trap is documented in this repo because an earlier version of this vertical fell into it.

Nationally the same basis gives €24 703 018 914 approved and €13 740 176 584 paid across 395 918 projects, an execution rate of 55.62%. Évora holds 0.67% of the national approved total and executes it below the national rate. One thing to know about that denominator: €3 694 494 765 of the national total — 14.96% of it — belongs to no concelho at all, recorded as national or with no location, so the share of place-attributable money is slightly larger than the figure above.

Status is judged against the snapshot's own reference date, not today's, because that is the date the source speaks for.

Now the caveat that has to travel with the first row of that table. 1 011 of the 1 445 locations counted as on time record an actual completion date falling exactly on the planned date — 69.97% of them. A modal difference of zero at that scale is more consistent with a field being closed out administratively than with delivery being observed and dated (inference). The remaining 434 locations record an actual date earlier than the planned one, and the same doubt about how these fields are filled applies to them too.

Two things sharpen that reading, and one of them stops it being a criticism of Évora. First, 470 of those exact matches are rows where the start date, the planned completion date and the actual completion date are all the same day. A project cannot be observed to begin and finish on one date at that scale, so those rows record a filing convention rather than an observed date (inference). All of them sit in a single investment line. Second, the pattern is national, not local: across the country 177 237 of 246 510 on-time locations carry the same exact-date stamp, 71.90%, which is slightly higher than Évora's. Nothing here says Évora reports differently from anywhere else; it says the register's completion dates cannot be read as observed delivery anywhere. So the on-time count is soft evidence, and quoting it bare would overstate punctuality by a wide margin.

The overdue group is the one that reads as a broken promise: a planned completion date already passed at the snapshot date, with no actual completion date recorded. In Évora that is 606 project-locations carrying €102 711 704 of approved money — 61.64% of everything approved for the concelho — of which €40 727 682 has been paid.

It is concentrated. Habitação has 39 project-locations in Évora, all of them past their planned date with nothing recorded as delivered: every one of them overdue. Two of the five largest project-locations in the concelho are in that state and have received nothing at all — the student residence at €7 483 162 approved and €0 paid, and the hospital equipment purchase at €5 314 868 approved and €0 paid.

That last one is worth naming plainly, because it crosses the two halves of this document. The equipment is for the new central hospital of the Alentejo; the money is approved, the planned date has passed, and nothing has been disbursed (inference: the source records approval, date and payment, and does not say why).

The grouping rule is ours, not the register's: fold accents, drop punctuation, drop the trailing legal-form abbreviation. It is stated so it can be disagreed with, and it is in Technical Source/fetch_base.py. What was audited

This section says what was audited and when. It does not say what the Tribunal found. The Tribunal de Contas publishes a catalogue — the title and abstract of each report — and separately the reports themselves as PDFs. What was fetched is the catalogue: 2 206 documents across 4 product families, spanning 1997 to 2026. Those 4 are not everything the Tribunal publishes — a limit this document measures rather than mentions, below. The conclusions are in the PDFs and were not read.

Place, in this source, is free text. There is no entity field, no concelho field, no NUTS field: a municipality reaches an audit only as words in a sentence. So matching is string matching, and every match below carries a confidence tier and the source's own words, because the bare count would be misleading. Why a silence here is not a silence

Two blind spots, both measured rather than assumed, and both first found by an agent that was forbidden to read this document.

The four families are not the Tribunal's output. They are four of the product families it publishes. A fifth — Relatórios de Apuramento de Responsabilidades, 192 documents — contains 2 reports whose subject is the Município de Évora itself:

  • Relatório nº 1/2016 - 1ª Secção — «Auditoria ao Município de Évora para o apuramento de responsabilidade financeira sancionatória identificada no âmbito do contrato de abertura de crédito na modalidade de “curto prazo” outorgado com o Millennium BCP»
  • Relatório nº 8/2013 - ARF — «Contrato de empreitada “Conceção/Construção da Escola EB1/JI dos Canaviais” celebrado entre o Município de Évora e o Consórcio “Ecociaf – Construção Civil e Obras Públicas, Lda. e Certar – Sociedade de Construções, S.A.” (Processo de fiscalização Prévia nº 1968/2009)»

So the true count of catalogued Tribunal documents about this municipality is at least three, not one, and the four-family search cannot see two of them.

The catalogue indexes abstracts, not report bodies. A thematic audit of how mainland municipalities fund their fire brigades carries no mention of Évora in its catalogue entry at all — and names the concelho 13 times in its own text, including its position in the Tribunal's own tables. That report sits inside a family this document searched. The search still could not find it.

The honest statement is therefore narrow: in the four families this document reads, nothing published after 2019 names Évora in its title or abstract. Anything stronger — that Évora has not been examined, that the recovery years went unaudited — is refuted by the two probes above.

Évora is an unusually clean name to search for: the matcher raises 0 structural risks against it, that is, no structural over-match risk. It is not one of the two Portuguese municipality names that belong to two places at once, and it is not a whole word inside a longer municipality's name. The gazetteer used to establish that is INE's own live list of 308 municipalities. Names that fail those tests behave very differently — a search for "Guarda" in this catalogue returns the national guard, not the city.

What the EU data cannot tell you

There is no EU figure for the concelho of Évora. Not "we could not find one" — the granularity does not exist in the source.

The probes, for the record. The EU's project-level dataset places 2 569 projects in the NUTS3 region containing Évora, which it names Alentejo Central. All 2 569 of them carry the programming period 2014-2020 — an earlier funding cycle, and a different instrument from the recovery plan whose figures open this document. Putting the two side by side would be comparing different things. The dataset has no concelho field, and for Portugal the postal code is empty on every row.

The size of the aggregation problem is the point. Alentejo Central contains 14 municipalities, per INE's own geographic dimension: Alandroal, Arraiolos, Borba, Estremoz, Montemor-o-Novo, Mora, Mourão, Portel, Redondo, Reguengos de Monsaraz, Vendas Novas, Viana do Alentejo, Vila Viçosa, Évora. A figure for that region is a figure for all of them.

A codes footnote that has cost this repo time before: PRR publishes the older NUTS vintage — the same dimension carries 25 NUTS3 entries — while the EU project data uses a newer one. Both call the region Alentejo Central; the codes are not interchangeable, and passing one to the other returns zero rows with a perfectly healthy HTTP status.

What to make of it

Everything above is the record; this section is a reading of it, added at the owner's request and signed by its author: it was written by the model that assembled the document, and it is the part that goes past what the sources state. Every figure in it repeats a ledger row already printed above; the connections and the emphasis are interpretation, and the sentences that interpret carry the same (inference) label the rest of the document uses. It stays on this side of the line the first section draws — where the money and the risk sit is a reading of registers; whether anyone managed well or badly would need the audits this document does not open.

Start with the share that is late, then decide what the dates are worth. Overdue project-locations carry 61.64% of everything approved for the concelho, and the completion dates that would soften that reading are the register's weakest field: 1 011 of the 1 445 on-time locations land exactly on their planned date. A date like that is a keystroke; a disbursement is a transaction. Where the two kinds of field disagree, believe the payments (inference).

Two promises in particular have the shape of trouble. The student residence and the hospital equipment purchase are among the five largest project-locations in the concelho, both are past their planned dates, and both stand at €0 paid against €7 483 162 and €5 314 868 approved. Under the reading above these are the credible red flags, because the payment column — the harder field — is the one saying nothing has moved (inference).

The accountability address is mostly not the town hall. The recipient tables above already say it: the university holds €38 596 976 contracted against the municipality's €12 069 013, and the layer that administers the money is made of national agencies. A reader who wants this total explained should be putting questions to national bodies and to the university more often than to the câmara (inference).

The registers are individually open and collectively unjoinable, and that is a finding, not a footnote. Inside one register, a hospital group needed 3 spellings folded together before it showed as 282 contracts worth €29 580 141; across registers, the same body appears under names a join would miss, and a search for the housing institute returned a zero that a verifier had to expose as false. The blame does not sit on missing identifiers — the recovery register carries a tax number on its entity rows, and the contracts register surrenders one contract by contract — but on where they sit: the contracts search returns names without the number, and the audit catalogue has no entity field at all. Follow-the-money work therefore fails at the seams between sources before it fails inside any one of them (inference).

The searchable audit trail is thinnest where the money is thickest. In the four families read, nothing published after 2019 names Évora in its title or abstract — and those are the years in which the recovery money moved. The document's own probes forbid the strong reading: the catalogue cannot see inside report bodies, and a fifth family holds 2 reports on the municipality that the four-family search cannot reach. What survives is the practical point: a citizen who goes looking for an examination of those years will not find it through this catalogue's search, whatever may in fact have been examined (inference).

If this document earns a sequel, the data says point it at health. The equipment for the new central hospital of the Alentejo sits approved and undisbursed past its date. In the contracts window, the health group stands as the most active contracting entity in the window read once its spellings are folded together. In the audit table above, hospital care in the concelho last appears through a 2019 report on managers' pay. Money, delivery risk and a thin searchable trail concentrate in the same corner of the same town (inference).

What would turn this reading into knowledge is short and concrete. Read the Tribunal PDFs this document links, which is where conclusions actually live; put the council's own accounts against DGAL's municipal-finance data, which the next section names as the missing source; and keep the weekly snapshots, because the register erases its own history and a trend will exist where these observations accumulate. Until then the honest landing is the one this section has tried to give: not a verdict, but a map of where verdicts should be sought first (inference).

What this document does not answer

  • Whether Évora's public money was well spent. Nothing here reads an audit's findings. The Tribunal's conclusions exist; they were not fetched.
  • How much the Tribunal has examined Évora. This document searched four product families by title and abstract, and can name a floor, not a total. Two of its own probes show the floor is well below the truth.
  • Why the overdue projects are overdue. The source records approval, dates and payment. It does not record reasons.
  • What the council itself did. Most of this money moves through national agencies and other public bodies. A council-level accounting question needs DGAL's municipal finance data, which this document does not use.
  • How Évora compares with a similar municipality. Contract totals cannot be compared across concelhos without double-counting multi-place contracts, and the PRR comparison would be a separate exercise.
  • What changed over time. The recovery plan overwrites its own snapshot every week. The observations recorded by this repo start in July 2026, so a trend does not exist yet — only the beginning of one.
