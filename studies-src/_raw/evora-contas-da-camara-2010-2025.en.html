The accounts of the Câmara de Évora, 2010 to 2025: the budget, what was collected, the debt and the certification

In brief

What does the Câmara de Évora budget, collect, pay and owe, and how did the debt inherited from the rescue get to where it is? This study reads the municipality's accounts from 2010 to 2025 in three voices: the municipality's own reports, the auditor's signed opinion and the independent debt series the regulator publishes.

In 2025 the council budgeted €109 483 315 and collected €67 263 297, or 61.44% of the budget; in 2021 it collected 96%. Total debt closed the year at €54 379 035, inside the legal limit and with €28 192 653 of headroom. The squeeze is on suppliers: 137 days, on average, to pay an invoice, and €4 976 172 in overdue payments. The 2024 accounts were rejected by the council itself and were never certified.

What this project concludes

The legal debt has been falling for a decade, and the squeeze has moved elsewhere. The regulator's debt index went from 242.6% in 2014 to 105.5% in 2024, and Évora came back under the legal ceiling in 2020. Suppliers saw the opposite: current debt to suppliers rose 70.20% in a year, and the average payment time went from 22 days in 2023 to 137 in 2025, with no consolidation loan to buy relief, as the loan of 2013 did, and those of 2016 and of 2019. Part of the room to keep paying down the past is being taken from the patience of today's suppliers.

A budget is not money. Revenue execution fell from 96% in 2021 to 61.44% in 2025, and the mechanism is in the accounts themselves: the municipality budgets outside money before anyone has committed it, such as the recovery-plan works and the protocol for access roads to the new hospital, and then that money does not come in. The 2026 plan does it again: the largest allocation recovered from its annexes, the Rossio de São Brás, carries €9 500 000† for the year, with €2 350 000† of defined financing.

When revenue disappoints, investment is what gives. In 2025 staff costs executed at 96.55%, loan amortisation at 93.64% and investment at 21.13%. The first two are contractual; the third is the one that gets postponed.

The inherited debt took years to measure: €82 871 523 two weeks after the 2013 change of executive, and €95 082 510 in the last restatement, as older liabilities surfaced. The excess over the legal ceiling, €32 559 910 in January 2014, was gone by 2020.

One year of this municipality's accounts exists without an outside signature. The 2024 accounts were rejected in the council by 5 votes against 2, and the auditor declared it could not certify them. The clean opinion on 2025 brings the outside witness back from here on, not backwards.

What the documents do not allow anyone to know: why the debt the reports print and the debt the regulator publishes sit four or five million euros apart from 2020 to 2023; what value the 2026 budget actually gives Évora 2027, which three documents write in three ways; and what the 2024 accounts would say, which the municipality never published.

What could work better

Budgeting as revenue the outside money that is already committed, or writing beside each line how much of it is secured: that gap is what explains the fall in execution. Publishing the 2024 accounts, or saying publicly why they do not exist. Publishing the annexes of the plan and the budget as text, not as images, so that each project's lines can be read. And the municipality and the regulator explaining the difference between the two debt series from 2020 to 2023.

This part is O Estado do País's reading from the documents of this study, and from them alone; every sentence points to a section of the document. The quoted facts are in the sections that follow, with the document and the page.

The limits of this document

The Prestação de Contas is management's account of the year. It is detailed, internally consistent and externally audited — but it is one voice. Two outside voices exist: the independent auditor's signed opinion (the Certificação Legal das Contas), and DGAL — the Direção-Geral das Autarquias Locais — which publishes, per municipality and per year, the same legal debt concept the report uses, compiled on the regulator's side.

A budget is not money. Évora's 2025 budget says €109 483 315 after revisions; the money that actually arrived was €67 263 297. Reading the budget as spending is the single easiest way to be wrong about a municipality, and the gap here is not small.

The 2024 accounts were never certified, and the 2024 figures are the ones the 2025 report prints as comparatives. The section on the auditor says why.

The national yardstick is one year behind, and its full study is not public. The Anuário Financeiro dos Municípios Portugueses 2024 — an academic publication (CICF/IPCA, with the accountants' order), not a government source — openly publishes only its conclusions presentation, and its national debt table stops at accounts year 2023. DGAL's per-municipality file reaches 2024.

"Debt" does not mean the same thing in every year of the table. Before the 2013 finance law, the legal concept was endividamento líquido — net debt — calculated in each year's management report; after it, the reported measure is dívida total de operações orçamentais, total debt of budgetary operations, which includes the municipality's share of the entities it participates in. The municipality also publishes a third figure, dívida global apurada, and a fourth, dívida orçamental without the participated entities. For several years two or three of these coexist in the same report with different values.

The accounting system changed underneath the series. The municipality kept its accounts under POCAL for the first part of the period and under SNC-AP for the later part. The 2021 report says in its own words that the new system makes results look worse than the old one, because of how it treats depreciation and amortisation. Results before and after the transition are therefore not one series, and the result column of the finance table should be read as two.

One year of accounts was published as scans, and one year was not published at all. Every 2020 and 2024 figure below therefore comes from the comparative column of a later report.

An asterisk in the tables means the figure comes from a later report's comparative column, not from a document of its own year. It is the same municipality reporting, but a year later, in a report written by the same executive about a year whose own accounts were never approved — for 2024, they were rejected. Five years carry it in part or in whole.

A dagger means the figure comes from a degraded scan. It appears in the section on the 2026 plan and nowhere else, and it is the same kind of marker as the asterisk. The plan's report body extracts cleanly; its investment annexes, where the project lines live, are a scanned layer in which digits and designations are frequently unreadable. Every figure taken from that layer carries a dagger. Lines that could not be recovered were left out; they are gaps, not zeros.

This study shows the accounts and the series, and attributes them to nobody: the adjustment programme, the pandemic, the transfer of powers to municipalities and the national economic cycle move the indices whoever sits in the council. The decisions with an author, and the elections that decided who took them, are in the study Who governed the Câmara de Évora, 2009 to 2025.

The initial 2025 budget was €104 000 000. One modifying revision — folding in the prior year's closing balance of €1 997 375 and budgeting €3 485 940 for the PRR-financed "Évora 2027" works — raised it by €5 483 315, to €109 483 315.

Two absences explain much of the 2025 gap, and the report names both. A revenue line of 5 million euros for "Évora 2027" saw no execution in the year. A technical-cooperation line of €5 013 000 — five million euros of it the protocol for road access to the new central Alentejo hospital — also saw none. Transfers from co-financed projects were budgeted at many times what arrived: €1 087 287 came in, an execution of 11.58% on that line. Budgeting money that outside bodies have not yet committed, and then not receiving it, is the recurring mechanism behind the falling execution series (inference; the report states each line, not the pattern).

This recital's window opens in 2018. The 2015 report carries 48.1% — a deeper trough, in the year after the bailout instalments — so execution collapses have followed Évora's debt crises before (inference).

The year closed with €2 381 077 in hand to carry into 2026.

Where it went

Current spending was €54 575 386, an execution of 70.64%. Two lines are 56% plus 34% of it:

Personnel executes at 96.55% because salaries are paid whether or not the rest of the budget happens; it is the incompressible core of the accounts. The goods-and-services line is where the large running contracts live — bulk water, sanitation, waste, public lighting — and it is also where the unpaid bills accumulate.

Capital spending was €10 989 664 — an execution of 34.10% — and its composition is the sharpest single fact in the accounts: of €24 159 745 budgeted for investment proper, the municipality managed to pay €5 104 307 — an execution of 21.13%. The report blames delays in PRR-expected projects and public tenders that attracted no bidders. Meanwhile €4 308 783 went out in loan repayments, at 93.64% of that budget line — the debt was serviced almost exactly as planned while investment ran at a fifth of its plan. Loan repayment is the one line of capital spending that cannot slip (inference; the report prints both rates without comparing them).

At year end, €25.7 million of commitments carried into 2026, of which €14.6 million were already invoiced and awaiting payment.

The revenue column is not one concept across the whole period. For 2010 to 2014 it is budgetary revenue received as the cash-flow statement prints it; from 2015 it is the total of the budget-execution table, which in most of those years explicitly includes the previous year's carried balance. The two are close but not identical, and the difference is the balance carried in.

Three of the years in the revenue column are loan years. The 2013 figures carry the support-programme instalments, the 2016 figures the financial-recovery loan, the 2019 figures the substitution loan. In each of those three years the revenue column steps up and the following year gives the step back, which is what a loan looks like in a cash-basis table and not what a good year looks like (inference; the reports state each loan and each total, not the pattern).

The personnel column steps up sharply between 2021 and 2023, and the 2023 report gives the reason and dates it. Competences in education, health and social action were transferred to the municipality, education taking effect in April 2022; the education transfer alone added €3 856 585 of current transfers in 2022 against 2021, and the report says plainly that revenue arriving from transferred competences implies a corresponding increase in spending.

The result column is two series, not one, because of the POCAL to SNC-AP transition, and only one positive year appears in it.

The management reports for 2010 to 2014 all have corrupted text layers — the fonts carry no character map, so a text extraction returns unreadable glyphs. What survives for the early years are the separately published cash-flow and income statements, which is why the first three years of the finance table have three columns filled and five empty.

One number in the table needs its footnote printed beside it. The 2016 result of minus €3 337 288 includes, as a cost of 2016, VAT relating to the years 2009 to 2011 — €1 712 963. The report writes the subtraction of the two and states as the corrected figure a loss of €1 984 259, but the subtraction it prints does not give that figure. Both results are the municipality's own: the table uses the first, the net result for the year, because it is what the accounts record and the same measure as the other years; the corrected one stays as the report writes it, with no arithmetic that explains it.

Two of the years of the 2017 to 2021 mandate are the pandemic years, and the 2020 figures in the finance table all carry the comparative asterisk because the published 2020 dossier has no text. Reading the investment and revenue numbers of 2020 and 2021 as a verdict on the executive would be a category error.

The 2025 accounts straddle the handover: they cover a year in which the executive elected in 2021 governed until 2025-10-31 and the next one from that date. They are printed in the finance table as a single year, because that is how they exist, and they are attributed to neither mandate.

The 2022 report explains that step itself, in the paragraph immediately above its own table. The increase over the previous year is attributed there, in the report's words, mainly to the integration of the non-teaching school staff carried out under the transfer of competences in education — a total of 309 workers, to which, it says, the year's admissions, returns and departures are then added, which is why the net change in the table is not the same figure. So the personnel step in the finance table and the headcount step here are the same event, and it is the municipality's own reports that make the link, on the spending side and on the staffing side alike.

The same pages disagree with themselves once. The 2022 report states that 203 workers left during that year, and then lists the departures by career on the same page; those five career figures add up to 201. The difference of two workers is not explained anywhere in the report and is not reconciled anywhere else in it. The 2023 report's equivalent list adds up to the total it states.

One further difference between the two reports is typographic and nothing more: the 2022 report prints its own 2022 headcount cell without the thousands space it uses on every other row of the same table, and the 2023 report's reprint restores it. The value is identical in both, and no year of the series was restated between them.

The debt, in three layers

Suppliers: the layer that is growing

Debt to suppliers ended 2025 at €19 821 418 — up €3 674 532 on the year, 22.76%. The layer to watch is inside it: ordinary current supplier debt went from €6 478 008 to €11 025 289 in one year — a rise of 70.20%. The report attributes it, in its own words, to the financial difficulties the municipality has felt especially since 2023. A quarter of that current debt is the monthly water invoice to Águas do Vale do Tejo alone. Beside it sit €5 642 725 of old water debt, restructured into two long-dated repayment plans whose creditor is now the European Investment Bank, and €3 153 404 owed to investment suppliers.

Two hard numbers measure the strain. The average time Évora takes to pay a supplier reached 137 days — up 33 days in a year:

Banks: the layer that is legacy

Medium and long-term bank debt ended the year at €29 418 657, down €3 549 470 on the year, across seven loans and three banks. Its composition is its history: 49% refinances debt from the PAEL — the state's municipal financial-support programme — and 42% is the 2016 financial-recovery (saneamento financeiro) loan. Together the rescue-era loans are 91% of the bank debt; 6% funds new investment. Servicing all of it cost €5 365 896 in 2025 — €4 308 783 of principal and €1 057 113 of interest.

Servicing the medium and long-term loans cost €4 877 620 in 2021 alone.

The total, against the legal ceiling

The legal measure — total debt of budgetary operations under Lei n.º 73/2013 — ended 2025 at €54 379 035: €54 261 280 the municipality's own, €117 754 its share of associated entities. The law caps that total at one and a half times the average net current revenue of the previous three years — for Évora, a ceiling of €82 571 687 on an average of €55 047 791 — so 2025 closes in compliance, with €28 192 653 of headroom.

The two land within a thousand euros of each other in 2024 and within a few hundred thousand in the years to 2019, with 2018 the exception in the other direction. From 2020 to 2023 they stand four to five million euros apart.

No document read explains the gap from 2020 to 2023: neither the reports nor the regulator's file say where the four to five million euros that separate the two series in those years come from. It is left unknown, and that is why this study always says which series it means when it says the debt rises or falls.

The national yardstick makes the same point from the other direction: the Anuário Financeiro's presentation — academic, not government, and for accounts year 2023 — puts the national municipal debt index at 39.8%, and counts 12 municipalities over the legal limit in 2023, a list on which Évora does not appear. Évora is legal, and it is also one of the most indebted municipalities in Portugal relative to its revenue. Both are true.

What the auditor signed — and what could not be signed

The Certificação Legal das Contas for 2025, signed 2026-04-17 by Marques, Cruz & Associados, SROC, is a clean opinion: the financial statements present the municipality's position truly and appropriately, and the budgetary statements comply with public-accounting norms. It carries emphases — matters flagged without qualifying the opinion: a bank reconciliation clean-up begun in 2025 and due to finish in 2026; the de-recognition of the low-voltage electricity-grid concession assets under a 2025 accounting instruction; some 2.8 million euros of creditor balances that are in substance non-current; and the existence of the arrears.

Under "other matters" sits the sentence with the longest shadow: the proposal for the 2024 accounts was rejected in the Câmara meeting of 2025-05-28 — 2 votes in favour, 5 against — and the auditor therefore issued a Declaration of Impossibility of Legal Certification for 2024. A year of this municipality's accounts exists without an external signature. The 2025 opinion being clean means the books recovered their external witness; it does not certify 2024 retroactively.

The documents record the vote and do not say why each member voted as they did. There are two readings of it, and neither is a fact: the vote took place about five months before the October 2025 local elections; and the 2 votes in favour match the seats of the president's list in the council elected in 2021, which the study Who governed the Câmara de Évora, 2009 to 2025 counts. No 2024 accounts document was ever published, and every 2024 figure in this study is a comparative column of the 2025 report.

The books, briefly

Total assets are €212 967 096; total liabilities €63 735 757; net worth €149 231 339. The year's accrual result is a loss of €2 443 292, against a loss of €4 591 039 the year before — nearly halved. Before depreciation and financing the year is positive by €6 248 696. Total accrual income was €68 276 720 against €70 720 012 of expense. The municipal balance sheet is solvent many times over on paper; the pressure in these accounts has never been about net worth, it is about cash and time (inference).

The budget-balance rule of the finance law — current revenue must cover current spending plus average loan repayment — is met, with €6 151 351 to spare, as it has been in each year of the report's four-year window.

What the 2026 plan says

The mandate installed in 2025 cannot be assessed from accounts yet. Its first budget, though, exists and is public: the Grandes Opções do Plano e Orçamento for 2026, published by the municipality. Nothing in it has been executed, and the accounts that will test it are the 2026 ones.

Its annexes — the multi-year investment plan and the activities plan, where the project lines live — are a scanned layer in which digits and designations are frequently unreadable. Every figure below that came out of that layer is marked †. Lines that could not be recovered are absent rather than guessed.

The size of it. The plan sets revenue and spending at €110 840 577 for 2026, an increase of €6 840 577 on the 2025 estimate, which the document itself prints as 6.58%. Staff costs are €33 344 550 of that total. Transfers to the twelve freguesias are €1 531 067, or 19.01% of every transfer the municipality plans to make. And the property-tax rate is set at 0.37% in the plan, with a 30% reduction for the rural freguesias.

Budgeting money that outside bodies have not yet committed, and then not receiving it, is the mechanism the 2025 accounts, above, name for the falling execution rates of recent years. The 2026 plan inscribes it again (inference; the plan states each line and each financing split, and states no pattern).
