Poverty and inequality are not the same thing

In 2025, the share of people at risk of poverty or social exclusion was smaller in Portugal than in the Union. But the 20 % with the highest income received 4,86source · Eurostat times what the 20 % with the lowest income received, against 4,62source · Eurostat in the Union.

Both figures, Portugal’s and the Union’s, count the people in at least one of three situations: income below 60 % of their country’s median income, the one in the middle; severe material and social deprivation; or living in a household with very low work intensity. Each person counts only once.

The European Commission’s reference values

In 2025, Portugal was outside 4 of the European Commission’s 13 reference values and within 9. Outside: government debt, the international investment position, the three-year change in unit labour cost and the annual change in house prices.

  • Government debt

    It is everything general government owes, as a percentage of GDP, the value of everything the country produces in a year.

    Portugal is above the European Commission’s reference value, which is 60 %.

  • What the country owns abroad minus what it owes (net international investment position)

    It is the difference between what residents of Portugal own in the rest of the world and what they owe to it, as a percentage of GDP, the value of everything the country produces in a year.

    Negative means the country owes abroad more than it owns there.

    Portugal is below the European Commission’s reference value, which is −35 %.

  • Labour cost per unit of output (unit labour cost)

    It is how much the cost of labour per unit produced rose over three years: what is paid for labour divided by what it produces.

    Portugal is above the European Commission’s reference value, which is 9 %.

  • House prices, annual change

    In 2025 the prices of homes bought by households rose 17,6 %source · Eurostat on average.

    Portugal is above the European Commission’s reference value, which is 9 %.

The reference values Portugal was within

  • Portugal’s share of advanced economies’ exports (export market share)

    It is how much Portugal’s share of the exports of advanced economies changed over three years: the part that Portugal’s exports of goods and services make up of the total exports of OECD countries and of EU countries outside the OECD. Positive means Portugal gained share.

    Portugal is above the European Commission’s reference value, which is −3 %.

  • Debt of (non-financial) companies

    It is what companies owe in loans and debt securities, excluding financial companies, as a percentage of GDP, the value of everything the country produces in a year.

    Portugal is below the European Commission’s reference value, which is 85 %.

  • Household debt

    It is what households and non-profit institutions serving them owe in loans and debt securities, as a percentage of GDP, the value of everything the country produces in a year.

    Portugal is below the European Commission’s reference value, which is 55 %.

  • Credit flow to (non-financial) companies

    It is how much credit companies took on in a year, minus what they repaid, excluding financial companies and foreign direct investment, as a percentage of the debt they already had at the end of the previous year.

    Portugal is below the European Commission’s reference value, which is 13 %.

  • Credit flow to households

    It is how much credit households and non-profit institutions serving them took on in a year, minus what they repaid, as a percentage of the debt they already had at the end of the previous year.

    Portugal is below the European Commission’s reference value, which is 14 %.

  • Current account balance

    It is the difference between what Portugal received from the rest of the world and what it paid to it, in goods, services and income, averaged over the last three years and as a percentage of GDP, the value of everything the country produces in a year. Positive means the country received more than it paid.

    Portugal is between the European Commission’s reference values, which are −4 % and 6 %.

  • People who work or are looking for work (activity rate)

    It is how much the share of the population aged 15 to 64 that is active, working or looking for work, changed over three years, in percentage points. Positive means the active share grew.

    Portugal is above the European Commission’s reference value, which is −0,2 pp.

  • Prices compared with trading partners, including the exchange rate (real effective exchange rate)

    It measures the price competitiveness of Portugal against its main competitors, allowing for inflation and exchange rates, over three years. A rise is an appreciation: prices in Portugal increase relative to those of its partners and price competitiveness falls.

    Portugal is between the European Commission’s reference values, which are −3 % and 3 %.

  • Unemployment rate

    It is the share of people aged 15 to 74 who are out of work, among those who work or are looking for work.

    Portugal is below the European Commission’s reference value, which is 10 %.
