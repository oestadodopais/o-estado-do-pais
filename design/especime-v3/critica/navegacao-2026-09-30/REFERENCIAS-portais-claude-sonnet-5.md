Survey: how statistics portals organise content (fetched pages only)

## 1. Our World in Data — https://ourworldindata.org/
- Entry points (topic tiles, "Popular pages"): "CO2 Emissions", "Population Growth", "Life Expectancy", "Poverty", "Impacts of Food", "War & Peace", "Global Education", "Artificial Intelligence", "Democracy". Organised by subject/global problem, not audience or geography. Banner: "14,095 charts" across "126 topics".
- Topic page (https://ourworldindata.org/life-expectancy): distinct hub, not a repeat — own "Key Insights" block, ~43-chart gallery, line charts, maps, named country comparison ("Nigeria's life expectancy was thirty years lower than Japan's").
- Geographic profile (https://ourworldindata.org/country/portugal): rendered as a generic country search/filter tool, not a populated Portugal profile. No chart or peer comparison observed.
- Chart patterns seen: line charts, maps, one horizontal bar chart, one scatter plot, one "dot-and-arrow" chart. Ranked-bar/strip/small-multiples for peer comparison: not observed.
- Readability: one-sentence mission statement at top; plain-language definitions beside figures; historical-context sentences. Item limit per screen: not observed.

## 2. UK ONS — https://www.ons.gov.uk/
- Entry points: "Business, industry and trade", "Economy", "Employment and labour market", "People, population and community", "Taking part in a survey?" — by topic. Headlines are metric cards ("Employment rate: 75.1%", trend arrow), not charts.
- Topic page (https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy): distinct index, 9 sub-topics each with a one-line plain-language description (GDP defined as "the main measure of UK economic growth..."); no charts on the index.
- Geographic profile (https://www.ons.gov.uk/explore-local-statistics/): opens on a "Find an area" search box ("postcode, local authority, region, parliamentary constituency...") covering "111 indicators". What a populated area profile shows, and its comparison format: not observed (fetch did not reach one).
- Chart patterns: not observed.
- Readability: plain-language phrasing on cards; explicit "Show more.../Show fewer..." toggle limiting the list.

## 3. Pordata — https://www.pordata.pt/
- Entry points: "Estatísticas" (Portugal/Municípios/Europa), "Quadros Resumo" (same three), "Retratos" (Europa/Municípios/"5 Décadas de Democracia"/Eleições Presidenciais), "Simuladores" (Inflação), "Publicações" — geography first, then product/data format.
- Municipality hub (https://retratos.pordata.pt/): opens on "Conheça e compare os dados do seu município" with search/map, then "Sabia que:" single-fact superlative cards per municipality (e.g. "Amadora é o município com mais residentes por km²"). No populated individual-municipality profile or comparison chart observed.
- Country page (https://www.pordata.pt/portugal/quadro+resumo/portugal-827713): distinct hub, one large table, 80+ indicators across six years (1981-2024), unit and short definition per row. No charts; no international/regional comparison.
- Chart patterns: not observed (all three pages fetched were text/table-based).
- Readability: one-line facts on homepage cards ("Portugal tem hoje 11.424.031 habitantes"); short plain-language subtitles on indicator rows.

- "Articles by theme" page (.../index.php?title=Articles_by_theme): distinct theme index — "Agriculture, fisheries, and forestry", "Economy and finance", "Environment and energy", "Population and social conditions", "Science, technology, and digital society", "International trade", "General and regional statistics", "Industry, trade, and services" — no per-article summaries.
- One theme link, "Income and living conditions" (.../index.php?title=Income_and_living_conditions), led to a further sub-index of roughly 60 articles, not a content article. Within the two-link budget, no chart, country table or key-points box was reached. The nesting itself (index to theme index to sub-index) is an observed fact.
- Chart patterns / geographic profile / readability: not observed on the three pages fetched.

## Patterns seen more than once
- Topic-led (not audience-led) front-page organisation: Our World in Data, ONS, CBS, INE, Statistics Canada.
- Geography as one nav entry among topic entries, not the primary front door: ONS ("Explore local statistics" sits apart from the topic nav), CBS ("Regional" is one of six items), Statistics Canada ("Geography" is one of eight items plus a facet). Pordata is the counter-example: geography is the first-order split, ahead of product type.
- Topic/section pages that are distinct hubs, not repeats of front-page content: Our World in Data (life-expectancy), ONS (economy), Pordata (Quadro Resumo), Eurostat (Articles by theme), CBS (Regional dossier), INE (municipalities folder) — six of eight sites.
- A geography entry point that opens on a hub, search box, or news list rather than a populated individual-place profile, on the page fetched: ONS, Pordata (retratos hub), CBS (regional dossier), INE (municipalities folder).
- Plain-language definitions placed directly beside a number or technical term: Our World in Data, ONS, Pordata.
- Bounded/limited lists as an explicit readability device: ONS ("Show more/Show fewer" toggle), Eurostat ("Top 10 most visited articles", exactly 10 shown).
- Not observed anywhere: the reader-favoured "27-country strip" (lowest, highest, EU average, Portugal's place) did not appear on any fetched page, on any of the eight sites.
