# Cold read: the sentences in which the site argues for itself

You are a cold reader from a different model family than the authors of these texts. You have read-only access to this package folder and nothing else. The site is O Estado do País, a Portuguese observatory written by AI under a published policy. Its rule for reader pages is that nothing explains itself; three places are allowed to explain the project, once: the About page (`src/data/sobre.mjs`), the Method page (`src/data/metodo.mjs`), and the receipt of a row (the fixed strings in `src/i18n/strings.mjs`, rendered as in `recibo-mourao.html`). The corrections page (`src/data/correcoes.mjs`), the Agenda notes (`src/data/agenda.json`), the declared changes (`src/data/mudancas-do-projeto.mjs`), the measure definitions (`src/data/figuras.mjs`) and the AI label texts (`src/data/politica-ia.mjs`) also carry sentences the project writes about itself or about what it does.

The director found this sentence on the About page and read it as the site justifying itself: «Quem exerce um cargo público é nomeado nos seus atos públicos, a partir dos documentos oficiais, no que fez bem e no que fez mal; ninguém é nomeado com base em rumores.» The rule in it is the first clause. «No que fez bem e no que fez mal» is an assurance of balance; «ninguém é nomeado com base em rumores» is a denial of an accusation nobody made. Both are the site arguing for itself. The plain form is: «Quem exerce um cargo público é nomeado nos seus atos públicos, a partir dos documentos oficiais.»

## Your one question

For every sentence in these texts that speaks about the project, its pages, its method, its sources, its choices or its people (in Portuguese and, where present, in English), decide: does it **state** (a rule, a fact, an instruction, a definition, a date), or does it **argue** (reassure the reader, deny a suspicion, praise the project's care, promise a virtue, explain a motive, contrast the project with what it is not, justify a choice, address an objection)? Report every sentence that argues.

Forms to look for, without limiting yourself to them: balance pairs («no bem e no mal», «tanto … como»); denials («ninguém», «nunca», «não … com base em», «não é … é», «sem …»); virtue words the trip list already names (`design/especime-v3/VOZ-MARCADORES.md`) and their cousins («cuidado», «exato», «independente», «sem edição», «tal como», «palavra por palavra» used as reassurance rather than as a rule); purpose clauses («para que …», «de modo a …») that explain why the project does something instead of what it does; motives («porque …») attached to rules; contrasts with imagined others («ao contrário de», «não é um portal», «não é opinião»); promises («compromete-se», «responde», «corrige à vista») when phrased as assurances rather than as procedures with a place and a date; and any sentence whose only job is to make the reader trust the site.

Do not report: rules stated plainly; definitions; procedures that say what happens, where and when (for example, how to send a correction); legal texts quoted; source excerpts; the names of things; dates and numbers.

## How to report

Write in English. Use exactly these sections, in this order: `## Argues`, `## Borderline`, `## States (fine)`. In `## Argues` and `## Borderline`, one numbered entry per sentence: the sentence quoted exactly as written (Portuguese, and the English twin if it exists), then `path:line`, then the form it takes in one clause, then the plain form you would keep (a rule, or nothing if the sentence has no rule in it). Group by file, About first. In `## States (fine)` give only counts per file (how many sentences about the project you read and kept as stating), so the direction seat knows the read was complete. End with three lines: how many sentences you read that speak about the project, how many argue, how many are borderline.
