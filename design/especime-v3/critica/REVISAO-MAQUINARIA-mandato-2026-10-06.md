# A review of the machinery of O Estado do País: where the time and the tokens go, and what can be cut without lowering quality, rigour or accuracy

You are reading, cold and read-only, the machinery of a site built and verified by AI agents. The folder you are in is a package: `scripts/` (every script of the site repository: the gates, the verify chain, the landing chain, the cold-read tools, the checkers), `package.json` (the npm scripts, including the `verify` chain), `CLAUDE.md` (the project's rules), and `registos/` with: the improvements register (`REGISTO-DE-MELHORIAS.md`), the repository map builders read (`MAPA-DO-REPOSITORIO-para-construtores.md`, which cites source lines by number), the CI workflow, the builder agent definition, the list and sizes of the test folders, the reports (`LEIA-ME.md`) and the gate logs (`verify.log`, `build.log`) and the cost files of six blocks built in the last twenty hours (C2, RP4-m, H3, RP4-c, R4, EX1), the last sixty issues, the decisions from §1.160 on, and the small scripts the editorial seat wrote during this session to repair the map's line citations after merging parallel branches (`acertar-citacoes-do-mapa-*.py`, `seguir-linhas-do-mapa.py`, `procurar-excertos.py`, `renumerar-issues.py`, `resolver-issues-h3.py`).

The owner's rule, which governs everything: the quality, the rigour and the accuracy of the content never go down, only up; efficiency is sought in everything else. The owner's complaint, which is the reason for this review: most of the hours go to machinery (gates, proofs, reports, merges, landings) and the reader-visible content does not improve at the same pace.

## What to measure and report

1. **Where the time goes.** From the six blocks' reports and logs: the wall time of a block, split into building, proofs and reports, gate runs, cold reads, fix passes, merges and landings (use the timestamps and the cost files; say what you could not measure). The duration of each step of the `verify` chain (the `verify.log` files carry the per-script output; the total is about 1 000 to 1 100 seconds) and which steps take most of it.
2. **Where the tokens go.** From the cost files: the share of a builder's effort spent on the proofs protocol (the `medidas.json` with a command and a known positive per number, the `conferir-relatorio.py`, the report's tables, the plants on the build, the captures at five widths and two editions), versus the code and content of the block.
3. **What protects a number, a source or a person, and what does not.** For each gate and checker in the `verify` chain and in the proofs protocol, say which category it is in, with the evidence (what it refuses, and whether a planted defect proves it bites). The rule of the house: a gate that protects a number, a source or a person stays; one that encodes furniture changes shape when the page changes.
4. **The cuts, ranked.** A ranked list of concrete changes that reduce time or tokens with no loss of protection, each with: what changes, the estimated saving (seconds per run, tokens per block, hours of the seat per week), the risk, and the measure that would prove no protection was lost (a planted defect the changed form still catches). Consider at least: the map's line-number citations (anchors or named markers instead of numbers; what the map is for and whether builders need it in that form); running only the checks a change touches between commits and the full chain once before landing, or in CI only; parallelising independent checks in the chain; the proofs protocol (which parts a reader of the report really uses; what a script could generate); the landing chain (local gates plus CI plus Vercel, about 65 minutes per block); inventories that are appended by hand and conflict on merge (the phrase inventory, the key list, the issue numbering, the audited readings JSON); the cold-read packaging (what the reader needs; the package sizes); anything else you find.
5. **What not to cut.** Say explicitly which parts earn their cost, with the evidence from the six blocks (for example, what the cold reads found; what the gates refused).
6. **The two-minute test for the owner.** In one paragraph a non-technical owner can read: what the machinery costs today per block, what it would cost after the cuts, and what stays the same for the reader.

## How to report

Write in English, under 2 500 words, in the sections above, with `path:line` references into the package for every claim about a script or a log. Mark every number as measured (with its file) or estimated. Do not propose cuts that remove a check whose refusal protects a number, a source or a person; if you think one such check is badly shaped, say how to reshape it with the same protection. Do not create files; return the review as your final message.
