# A review of the rules of O Estado do País: which rules protect the truth of what reaches the reader, which protect the process at a cost worth paying, and which are outdated or redundant

You are reading, cold and read-only, the rules under which a site is built and verified by AI agents. The folder you are in holds: `CLAUDE.md` (the project's rules, rewritten on 17.09.2026 and amended since), `VISAO.md`, the autonomy policy, the structure and vocabulary document, the improvements register (one entry per improvement, numbered M), the suggestions-box protocol, the builder and reader agent definitions, the common prompt of the cold reads, the ledger's README (the rules of a row), the repository map builders read, two recent briefs (each carries in its §6 the standing rules every block gets), the full decisions register since §1.112 (the rewrite), and the list of the observatory folder. The rules accumulated block by block over five weeks; some were born from one failure and may no longer apply to the project as it is now.

The owner's definition of what matters, which governs this review: nothing reaches the site that is not accurate, true and checkable. That is the whole of «accuracy and rigour». Everything else (process, form, records, machinery) is to be judged by its cost against what it protects.

## What to do

1. **Inventory the rules.** List every rule you find, in the order of the documents, each with: where it lives (`file:line`), what it demands, and the failure it was born from if the text says so.
2. **Classify each rule** into one of three: (A) it stops something untrue, inaccurate or uncheckable from reaching the reader (a number without a ledger row, a source misquoted, a sentence that contradicts the number, a person named outside the norm, a page that silently changed); (B) it protects the process at a cost worth paying (it prevents lost work, an unsafe landing, a leaked path or personal datum, a decision made by the wrong seat); (C) it is outdated, redundant with another rule, or furniture whose cost exceeds what it protects. Give the reason and the evidence for each classification. Where two rules say the same thing in two places, say which copy should stay.
3. **The rules that conflict or that the practice already ignores.** Point to rules that contradict each other, or that the six blocks of the last day did not follow (compare the briefs, the reports and the decisions), and say which side should win.
4. **The minimum set.** Write the shortest set of rules that still guarantees the owner's definition, in plain Portuguese, as a draft of a new `CLAUDE.md` section «As regras que não se quebram» (keep the existing register references where a rule has one). Then list separately the process rules worth keeping (B) with their cost, and the rules to retire (C) with the reason.
5. **The two-minute test for the owner.** One paragraph a non-technical owner can read: how many rules there are today, how many protect the truth of the site, how many are process, how many can go, and what changes for the reader (nothing should).

## How to report

Write in English, under 3 000 words, with `file:line` references for every rule. Mark every count as measured. Do not create files; return the review as your final message.
